Vinyl is back: how to buy your first record player

Record collections are all over TikTok, and vinyl keeps outselling expectations. Here’s how to get started without damaging your records or your budget.

By the ViralGadgets editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Jakob Rosen on Unsplash

On this page

  1. The three ways to set up
  2. Preamps, explained
  3. Bluetooth: handy, with a catch
  4. What to avoid

The three ways to set up

  1. All-in-one players with built-in speakers: cheapest and simplest, but the sound is limited.
  2. Turntable + powered speakers: the sweet spot for most beginners. Many entry turntables have a built-in preamp, so you can plug straight into powered speakers.
  3. Turntable + amplifier + speakers: the classic hi-fi route, more flexible and more expensive.

Preamps, explained

A record’s signal is very quiet, so it needs a phono preamp before it reaches an amplifier or powered speakers. Many beginner turntables have one built in (often switchable). If yours doesn’t, you’ll need a separate preamp or an amp with a “phono” input.

Bluetooth: handy, with a catch

Bluetooth turntables can stream to wireless speakers or headphones, which is convenient. Audio quality depends on the speaker, and some fans prefer a wired connection for the best sound.

What to avoid

Very cheap suitcase-style players are cute, but many have heavy tonearms and basic cartridges that can wear records faster. If you plan to build a collection, an entry-level turntable with an adjustable counterweight is a better long-term buy. See record players in our audio finder.
