ROG Xbox Ally explained: which model should you get?

Xbox’s first proper handheld arrived in October 2025, built with ASUS. It comes in two versions that look almost identical but are very different inside, and very different in price.

By the ViralGadgets editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Javier Martínez on Unsplash

On this page

  1. What it is
  2. Ally vs Ally X
  3. What reviewers said
  4. Should you buy one?

What it is

The ROG Xbox Ally is a Windows 11 gaming PC shaped like a controller, with a 7-inch 1080p 120Hz screen. What makes it different from earlier Windows handhelds is the Xbox full-screen experience: it boots into a console-style Xbox interface instead of the Windows desktop, so it feels more like a games console. It plays your PC games from Xbox, Game Pass for PC, Steam and other stores.

What reviewers said

The Ally X was widely praised for performance and comfortable grips, with Notebookcheck noting the Xbox interface is meant to make Windows easier to use on a handheld. The common catches: it’s expensive, and battery life in demanding games is short, as on most PC handhelds. A newer OLED version, the Ally X20, has since been announced at a higher price, according to Tom’s Guide.

Should you buy one?

  • Get the Ally X if you want to play big new PC games on the go and the budget is there.
  • Get the standard Ally for lighter games and Game Pass, but compare it with a Steam Deck first.
  • Get a Switch 2 if you mostly want Nintendo games or a family console.

Frequently asked questions

Is the ROG Xbox Ally an Xbox console?
No. It’s a Windows gaming PC with an Xbox-style interface. It plays PC games, including PC Game Pass, not Xbox console discs.
Can it play Steam games?
Yes. Because it runs Windows, you can install Steam and other PC game stores.
