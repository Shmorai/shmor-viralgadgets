Smart glasses explained: Ray-Ban Meta and beyond

Smart glasses have gone from sci-fi to the For You page. Here’s what they actually do, the difference between models, and the etiquette of wearing a camera on your face.

By the ViralGadgets editorial team · 6 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Oscar Nilsson on Unsplash

On this page

  1. What smart glasses do
  2. Glasses with a display
  3. Privacy and etiquette
  4. Who should buy them?

What smart glasses do

Most popular smart glasses, such as Ray-Ban Meta, look like ordinary frames but add a small camera for hands-free photos and point-of-view video, open-ear speakers for music and calls, and a voice assistant (Meta AI) you can ask questions. They don’t have a screen.

Glasses with a display

Meta Ray-Ban Display adds a small screen inside one lens for messages, directions and captions, and is controlled with a wristband. Meta paused its planned international rollout in early 2026 because of demand in the US, so check Meta’s or Ray-Ban’s site in your country for current availability.

Privacy and etiquette

  • A light on the frame shows when you’re recording, but people may not notice it. Ask before filming people close up.
  • Many venues, gyms, schools and workplaces don’t allow recording; take them off where cameras aren’t welcome.
  • Review the app’s privacy settings, including how AI features use your photos and voice.

Who should buy them?

Great for cyclists, runners, parents capturing moments hands-free, and creators filming point-of-view clips. If you mainly want audio, open-ear headphones are cheaper. Compare them in the phones and wearables finder.
